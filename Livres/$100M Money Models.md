# $100M Money Models

> **Auteur** : Alex Hormozi | **Année** : 2025 | **Genre** : Business / Stratégie d'offres & Money Models | **Source** : epub | **Mots lus** : 41 031 | **note générée le** : 2 août 2026 | **Une phrase** : "A Money Model is a deliberate sequence of offers" - l'ordre, le contenu et la manière dont vous enchaînez vos offres déterminent si vous gagnez assez d'argent au cours des 30 premiers jours pour que le cash ne soit plus jamais un frein à la croissance.

## Vue d'ensemble

Troisième volume de la trilogie (après *$100M Offers* - quoi vendre - et *$100M Leads* - comment trouver les gens), ce livre répond à la question : "How do I get them to buy it?" - par un Money Model. Hormozi y organise TOUTES les offres possibles en **quatre types** : Attraction Offers (transformer des inconnus en clients), Upsell Offers (faire dépenser plus), Downsell Offers (transformer des "no" en "yes"), et Continuity Offers (fidéliser avec des revenus récurrents). Le livre expose 15 offres concrètes, chacune avec son histoire d'origine, ses mécaniques chiffrées, ses scripts verbatim et ses pièges, puis montre comment les combiner en un modèle en trois étapes (Get Cash → Get More Cash → Get The Most Cash). La démonstration finale est la trajectoire réelle de Gym Launch : de 0 à **$4,400,000/mois en 20 mois** grâce à l'ajout successif d'offres. L'objectif d'un *$100M Money Model* : faire plus de profit avec un client qu'il n'en coûte pour en acquérir et servir deux autres, **dans les 30 premiers jours**, supprimant ainsi le cash comme limiteur d'échelle.

## Les 10 idées maîtresses

1. **Un Money Model est une séquence d'offres** - "It's what you offer, when you offer, and how you offer it". Faire une offre vaut mieux que rien ; faire plus d'offres vaut mieux qu'une ; les séquencer fait un Money Model.
2. **La vitesse du profit est un levier** - doubler la valeur des clients, leur nombre et la vitesse d'encaissement = croissance **8x** ; tripler = **27x**. Un bon Money Model encaisse avant de dépenser.
3. **Les quatre types d'offres couvrent tout** - Attraction (attirer), Upsell (dépenser plus), Downsell (dire oui quand on aurait dit non), Continuity (acheter encore). Chacun a sa fonction dans la machine.
4. **L'attraction peut être gratuite si le back-end paie** - le propriétaire de storage fait **$127 sur un premier mois "gratuit"** ($47 de cadenas, $10/mois d'assurance, un cran de taille au-dessus) ; Hormozi faisait **$680/client avant même d'ouvrir** sa salle.
5. **L'upsell fait la différence entre "front end" et business** - McDonald's passe de $0.25 à $3.00 de profit (**11.6x**) par client avec trois upsells ; les clients qui n'ont rien à acheter ensuite s'en vont.
6. **Le downsell n'est jamais du discounting** - on change ce qu'ils reçoivent (Feature Downsells) ou comment ils paient (Payment Plans, Trial With Penalty). "If you're gonna give something, get something." Ne jamais baisser le prix "just because" : les clients parlent des prix.
7. **Les plans de paiement sont un pari** - 7 étapes, jusqu'à 9 offres, du paiement intégral au Free Trial ; la cadence de facturation pilote le churn (Profitwell, 14 000 entreprises : 10.7% mensuel vs 5% trimestriel vs 2% annuel).
8. **La continuité inverse la mathématique** - $10,000 aujourd'hui devient $2,000 maintenant + $40,000 dans le temps, avec 4x plus de clients à upseller ; le tout sans casser le profit à 30 jours si on séquence correctement.
9. **"Simple scales. Fancy fails."** - moins de 100 produits à offrir, plutôt 100 façons d'offrir le même produit ; on construit une étape à la fois, "measure in quarters, not weeks".
10. **Le cash n'est jamais le problème** - avec un *$100M Money Model*, le profit d'un client finance l'acquisition et le service de deux autres clients en 30 jours : "A $100M Money Model eliminates cash as a bottleneck for growth. Mission accomplished."

## Chapitre par chapitre

### Start Here - l'origine (p.9)

**Concepts clés** : "A Money Model is a sequence of offers." Le problème du "drip" : "the slow drip of profits from lots of customers eventually pays for a single customer. This 'drip' starves the business of cash." Deux options : "Option #1: Wait two years to get paid and pray you don't run out of money. / Option #2: Get paid fast and grow as much as you darn well please. A good Money Model is option 2."

**Frameworks & méthodes** : Le Money Model du storage owner (7 leviers : mois gratuit → cadenas **$47** → cartons/marqueurs → déménagement affilié → assurance **$500 gratuite** upgradable à **$100,000 pour $10/mois** avec cadenas spécial → toujours proposer une taille au-dessus → total **$127/client**) ; le funnel gym de Hormozi : **20 leads/jour** via Free 6 Week Challenge → ~50% se présentent → 50% achètent un programme **$600** (~25% des leads) → **+$80** de suppléments = **$680/client** → coût lead **$5** = "put one dollar in…and get 34 dollars out…in 48 hours" → back-end : "they can get their $600 back as credit if they choose to sign up for a year. Two-thirds of sign-ups convert" → **$20,000/mois d'adhésions pour $3,000 d'apport**, nouvelle salle tous les 6 mois sans dette. Location de voiture : 5 offres en séquence ($19 → **$100/jour**, 5x). Math de croissance : 2x valeur × 2x clients × 2x vitesse = **8x** ; 3x = **27x**. La "death spiral" du mauvais modèle (8 étapes, de "spend money to get customers" à "they lose it all").

**Exemples & histoires** : la "concrete bedroom" sous le parking (réveillé par des "gunshots" de voitures sur les séparateurs métalliques) ; le mentor du storage (27 locations, "I have 27") ; la consult à **$25,000/heure** avec le marketer à "a million bucks a month" : "you have a level-10 skill in a level-2 opportunity" ; la trajectoire : 6 gyms, 9 mois pour sortir, 30+ turnarounds, licensing, **$43,000,000 de distributions en 5 ans**, vente de **66% à $46,200,000 en cash**, **$100M de net worth à 31 ans**, portfolio Acquisition.com à **$200M+/an**.

**Citations** : "I came for a $19/day car and I left paying $100/day. A 5x difference! And that's the power of a well designed Money Model." ; "Think of $100M Money Models as a book of winning lottery tickets-all you have to do is cash them in." ; "Also, I want to make something clear, these are my private notes. If it's here, I've made money with it."

**À retenir** : encaisser vite ; séquencer des solutions à des problèmes que le client vient de découvrir ; financer la croissance avec le cash client, pas la dette ; appliquer ses compétences au marché le plus gros possible.

### Section I - What's A Money Model ? & The Four Types of Offers (p.17-22)

**Concepts clés** : "Making one offer works better than making none. And making more offers works better than making one. Combining offers in a sequence makes a Money Model." Les 4 types : "1. Attraction Offers turn strangers into customers. 2. Upsell Offers get people to spend more cash. 3. Downsell Offers get people to say yes when they would have said no. 4. Continuity Offers keep people buying." La logique de l'ordre : sans offre d'acquisition → pas de clients ; sans upsell → trop peu d'argent ; sans downsell → des "no" perdus ; sans continuity → pas de cash garanti mois après mois.

**Frameworks & méthodes** : Les 7 règles préliminaires : 1) tous les business ont des Money Models - mantra : "how will I make this work for my business?" au lieu de "this won't work for my business" ; 2) certains modèles marchent mieux selon le business ; 3) rembourser sans discuter et réparer ses bêtises ; 4) "Hard Selling Is For Weak Products… It's a numbers game" ; 5) obéir à la loi (attention à "free") ; 6) "Be Transparent… Once you have a bad one [reputation], it sticks for life" ; 7) "Any Offer Can Be Used On Its Own, At Any Time, in Any Order."

**Exemples** : les blurbes satiriques ("Alex is my husband." - Leila Hormozi) ; les épigraphes : "Stop being poor. - Paris Hilton" ; "The limit does not exist. - Lindsay Lohan, Cady Heron in Mean Girls" ; Buffett : "Risk comes from not knowing what you're doing." ; Munger : "More important than the will to win, is the will to prepare."

**Citations** : "Attraction Offers generate leads and convert them into customers. They turn advertising into money by offering something free or at a discount. We do this because everyone wants a great deal." ; "People look for one thing and then buy another by accident all the time. Attraction Offers get them to do it on purpose."

**À retenir** : un grand deal = "customers get far more value than the price they pay" - et les inconnus ne comprennent que le prix, donc "the greater the discount, the better the deal. The greatest discount of all being free." Le continuum "free/discount/$1" est interchangeable.

### Win Your Money Back (p.29)

**Concepts clés** : formule "If you do x within y time within z rules, you can get it free." "Customers put money down. If they do the stuff OR they get the result OR both-they get it back as cash or store credit." Trois critères de remboursement : Results ("Making $X a month, Getting Y customers, Losing Z lbs"), Actions ("attend all sessions, calls, meetings, log progress, take pictures, do assigned homework"), ou Actions AND Results. Règle : "Only use a Win Your Money Back Offer if your refund rate is below 5%." Donnée secteur : "about 10% of all customers will ask for their money back."

**Frameworks & méthodes** : 3 caractéristiques des bons critères : 1) Easy To Track (les téléphones traquent déjà les pas, les caméras datent les photos), 2) Gets Customers Results ("make everyone do what the best customers do"), 3) Advertises The Business (posts, tags, avis, références). Application du store credit étalé : produit **$200/mois**, gains **$600** → ne pas donner 3 mois gratuits → **$600/12 = $50/mois de réduction → $150/mois** : "people fall off if they don't pay something." Les 3 rendez-vous des salles : Nutrition Orientation → "Before pictures" → offre suppléments ; Progress Check-in → offre adhésion ; Transformation Feedback → "After pictures" → offre adhésion + réduction pour prépayer un an. "Make Everyone A Winner" : vendre le programme comme conditionnel, puis à mi-parcours faire l'offre suivante "as if they already won" ; à la fin, laisser les "losers" gagner : créditer tout le dépôt vers le long terme. "Don't Take Blood Money" : "if a customer asks for a refund-entitled or not-I give it to them."

**Exemples** : Danny (juin 2013) : "I give you $500. You train me for eight weeks. And if I hit my goal, I get my money back… Fair enough?" - "I figured he wasn't gonna buy anyways, so I sold him." → 13 références grâce aux photos avant/après. Exemples d'offres : Free 28-Day Blueprint (B2C), "5 Customers In 5 Days" Challenge (B2B), "Put 1,000,000 Miles On Your Car, And Get A Free Car" (produit physique, offre réelle). "This offer has generated over $1 billion in sales industry-wide."

**Citations** : "Everyone thinks businesses make money on people who fail the program. No. The real money comes from people who succeed with it and you have something else to offer them." ; "we don't get customers to make a sale, we make sales to get customers." ; "My testing showed offering store credit and cash back got the same number of customers. So you might as well offer store credit."

**À retenir** : prévoir un upsell pour appliquer les gains avant de lancer l'offre ; viser les choses que les gens commencent et abandonnent (poids, skills, business, santé mentale).

### Giveaways (p.36)

**Concepts clés** : "advertise a chance to win a big prize in exchange for contact information and whatever else you want. Then, after picking a winner, you offer everyone else the big prize at a discounted price." Aussi appelés "scholarship", "sweepstakes", "raffles". Le Grand Prize = la chose que vous voulez que tout le monde achète, avec une valeur monétaire affichée comme **price anchor**. L'offre promotionnelle = le "partial scholarship" / "participation trophy". "The beauty is-everybody else qualifies for your promotional offer."

**Frameworks & méthodes** : Process en 6 étapes (choisir le Grand Prize, l'offre promotionnelle, collecter les contacts + critères d'éligibilité, les qualifying actions, une deadline, annoncer le gagnant). **Cost-to-value** : "make your core offer discount equal to 10%-30% of your gross margins" ; exemple : Grand Prize **$5,000 de valeur / $2,000 retail**, les autres achètent à **$1,800** (10% de réduction) → "a 10% discount becomes a 64% difference in cost-to-value!" Urgence à trois endroits - "to enter, to claim, to use" : entrée 3 à 7 jours, updates quotidiens, claim limité à **7 jours**, usage "I like hours, but I have gone up to five days", call le jour même ou lendemain. Scarcité : temps (7 jours) et/ou entrées (**5,000**), "I match how many people I let enter… with the number of people I have the time and resources to connect with inside seven days." Downsell au call : "they qualified for two prizes… present your promotional offer first… then offer the same discount by percentage on any other product." Récurrent : "Apply their discount over the longest period of time they'll agree to. Then… bill automatically at normal rates." **Deux prix** : "if someone they refer wins the grand prize, they win one too."

**Exemples** : la certification fitness (août 2020) : full-ride scholarship + partial scholarships ("Most of them join our program on the spot") ; Dentiste : braces **$6,000** / gift card **$2,000** ; Dog food : **$1,000** / **$300** avec abonnement 1 an ; Services : **$5,000** / voucher **$2,000** ; Consulting : **$12,000** / **$6,000** Partial Scholarship. Échec de portfolio company (billets d'événement) → succès avec un bundle équipement **$50,000** + produit cœur 1 an.

**Citations** : "Many will enter…many will win." ; "If Your Giveaway Doesn't Work, It Means Your Grand Prize Wasn't Grand Enough." ; "Remember, everyone that entered the Giveaway showed interest in your thing. And if somebody shows interest in a thing you have to offer-offer it to them."

**À retenir** : légalité ("somebody actually has to win the Grand Prize", règles écrites claires, plusieurs gagnants possibles, consulter un avocat) ; "the more work you make it to enter the fewer people will enter, but the more qualified they'll be-so find your sweet spot."

### Decoy Offer (p.44)

**Concepts clés** : "Decoy Offers advertise something free or discounted. Then, when leads ask to learn more, you also present a more valuable premium offer." L'Attraction Offer "only has to get leads to engage. Nothing more." Le "turkey talk" : "Say a Thanksgiving turkey takes three hours to cook… if you double the temperature to cook it in half the time-you burn it!" Permission de vendre : "Are you here for free stuff or lasting results?" Assumed close : "This is what everyone does. This is just a formality. Let me get your ID and credit card so you can get your value. No hype. Just friendly disposition. Almost bored over how regularly people buy." Surprise Benefit : "hey, I'm gonna throw this in, even though it's part of our premium offer…" ("Remember-they're still leads!")

**Frameworks & méthodes** : 2 étapes : 1) "Advertise a lesser, smaller, or simpler version of your premium offer as a decoy" (moins de composants, anciens modèles, moins personnalisé, **aucune garantie**) ; 2) "When leads engage, offer both options, but emphasize the premium one." Gestion du lead qui demande le decoy : comparer puis demander "which do you think will get you to your goal faster?" - ils doivent choisir le premium eux-mêmes. "Make The Contrast Huge." "Advertise Benefits Not The Features." **Show-up rate** : "If you run a Free Attraction offer, you'll get more leads. If you run a Discount Offer, you'll get fewer leads, but a higher percentage will show up."

**Exemples** : John et le 5-Day $5 VIP Tanning Pass (juin 2014) : "Why buy so many $25 day passes when members get unlimited access for just $19.99?" ; l'offre gym qui a sauvé la salle : option gratuite (1 session/semaine) vs "Ultimate" **$399** (sessions illimitées, coaching 1-1, garantie résultat ou répétition gratuite) → "We got about eight out of ten people to take the $399 option."

**Citations** : "You have to know what gets results for customers better than they do. This makes our premium offer the clear solution." ; "giving customers what they want now, so you can give them what they need later."

**À retenir** : "They either take the decoy version or the premium version… But either way, you can close everyone."

### Buy X Get Y Free (p.50)

**Concepts clés** : "when customers buy something, they get other stuff free. Free offers get way more attention than Discount Offers." "by selling more than one thing at once, you can turn Discount Offers into even stronger Free Offers." Quatre façons d'annoncer une réduction (25% off / $300 off / 3 Months Free / One Year For $900 ($1,200)) - "It's worth testing to see which one converts better in your market."

**Frameworks & méthodes** : Reframe t-shirt : "I could sell three t-shirts for $10 each for a total of $30 or I could sell one t-shirt for $30 and give two away free. It's the same price, but way more free stuff!" Ladder de prix équivalent : "Buy 12 Months Get 6 Months Free" / "Buy 9 Months Get 9 Months Free" / "Buy 6 Months Get 12 Months Free" - tous à **$1,800** : "(Hint: It has the most free stuff!)". Cash rapide sur clients existants : limiter à **10% des clients**. "So, permanently raise prices to accommodate the discount. Don't lie. Actually raise your prices." Ne pas dépenser l'argent des services à livrer : "Selling stuff you can't deliver on breaks the law and ruins your reputation."

**Exemples** : Boot Factory (Nashville, 2020) : "BUY 1 PAIR GET TWO PAIR FREE" - 3 paires de bottes à **$200** chacune, payées **$600** : "They charged three times the price for a single pair of boots because they came with two more pairs."

**Citations** : "If you only have one shot, you may as well make it count!" ; "After all, they may have prepaid months ago. Their wallets have been 'refreshed' with new money that is dying to make it into your pocket."

**À retenir** : plus de "free stuff" que de choses payées (buy one get two, pas buy two get one) ; viser les touristes/acheteurs uniques.

### Pay Less Now or Pay More Later (p.57)

**Concepts clés** : "you give people a choice to pay full-price later OR pay a discounted price now. This play works so well because we remove all risk from the customer." Pay Later : "advertise 'free'", carte enregistrée, cancel avant le débit ; Pay Now : "a 20-50% discount and greater bonuses". Conditional Satisfaction Guarantee : "People can only cancel the billing if they qualify" (ex. : assister à la formation). "Promise A Clear Yes/No Result" (ex. : douleur notée 1-10 avant/après).

**Frameworks & méthodes** : 4 étapes (offrir Pay Later → annoncer "free" → proposer Pay Now après l'acceptation → avoir un upsell prêt). Optimisation : "If too many people take your 'pay later' option, discount the 'pay now' option more, add better bonuses, or both." Règle : "If More Than 10% Of 'Pay Later' People Cancel Their Payment. You promised too much, the guarantee conditions are too low, or the price is too high." Version récurrente : "pay a higher ongoing rate 30 days later or they pay less today and keep the lower rate for good."

**Exemples** : formation lecture (juin 2016) : "Double your reading speed in 3 hours, or it's free" - carte à $0, **$297** débités demain, annulables par email avant, ou **$97** maintenant avec les enregistrements en bonus → il a payé les $297 ; Real estate workshop : **$500** later / **$299** now ; hedges : **$599** / **$369** + traitement + upsell **$199/mois** ; clothing : **$149** / **$97** + accessoire.

**Citations** : "Note: No matter how well you deliver, some people will cancel their payment. That's okay. Factor it in your costs of doing business and live your life."

**À retenir** : "Whether they choose to pay now or pay later, you've got customers, and likely, some profit."

### Free Goodwill Offer (p.62) & Conclusion Attraction (p.65)

**Concepts clés** : demander un avis en le cadrant comme une aide : "Would you help someone you've never met if it cost you nothing, but you didn't get credit?" - "Welcome to #mozination. You're one of us." Conclusion : les cinq Attraction Offers : Win Your Money Back, Giveaways, Decoy Offers, Buy X Get Y Free, Pay Less Now or Pay More Later. "They turned $1,000 into $10,000,000 in ten months because when I got returns I kept doubling down."

**Frameworks & méthodes** : instructions de review pas à pas par plateforme ("It takes less than 60 seconds to change someone's life forever.") ; enchaînement vers Section III : "we need to boost our 30-day profits by selling them more stuff."

**Citations** : "If you tell yourself you'll do it later, insead, please do it now."

**À retenir** : le but d'une Attraction Offer : "to turn strangers into customers… Ideally, we get enough cash to cover the cost of the customer and the cost to deliver our thing multiple times over."

### Section III - Upsell Offers & The Classic Upsell (p.66-69)

**Concepts clés** : "Upsells just mean whatever we offer next." "When an offer solves a problem, another appears. You upsell the solution to the problem your offer reveals. So, every offer opens the door to an upsell…even upsells!" Le Classic Upsell : "offers a solution to the customer's next problem the moment they become aware of it" - "You can't have a car without insurance. You can't have a car without gas. You can't have a good trip without a late checkout." Échecs d'upsell : offert trop différent, trop tôt ("before they've experienced the problem"), ou pas cru. Trois directions : "More of what they just got (think quantity)… Better versions of it (think quality)… New or complementary stuff (think different)."

**Frameworks & méthodes** : 12 notes clés : 1) "You barely have a business-you have a front end" (clients "5x'd their business because they actually offered an upsell") ; 2) offrir le produit le plus rentable d'abord ; 3) **Say No To Say Yes** : "You don't want anything [besides what I just offered] do you?" - "let the nos (yeses) roll in" ; 4) Surprise and delight : garder jusqu'à 4 bonus, les ajouter un par un, les donner tous même si oui immédiat ; 5) Hyper buying cycle : "weddings, starting new hobbies, having babies, moving to new places" ; 6) les bonus créent des problèmes que l'upsell résout ; 7) "A $10,000 thing you get later is worth less than a $10,000 thing you get now" - "put it in their hands before they've said yes. It's way harder to give something back than it is to say no" ; 8) nommer les bundles ("Fastest results", "Transformation package", "Minimum package") ; 9) intégrer l'upsell à la livraison ("Integrate the next thing you wanna sell into the first thing they buy") ; 10) BAMFAM : "Don't let them leave without booking!" (Sharran : "A customer should know the next time they see you-and why-before they leave.") ; 11) "Offer as many solutions as there are problems you can solve" ("The second worst thing that happens is they say no. The worst thing is if they would've said yes but you never asked.") ; 12) monétiser garanties/warranties/assurance : "just add 5-50% onto the price."

**Exemples** : le fourreur (été 2016) : "We advertise free earmuffs with coat storage… 'Great. And we'll store those as well for $30. You don't want to store anything else do you?' And of course, they say no." ; car wash → sealant ("You're not gonna wanna do the wash without sealant") ; vélo → casque → lumières → pneus ("You can't have a bike without a helmet!") ; cours d'exercice → cours de nutrition ("You can't out-exercise a bad diet") ; l'art studio : +10% pour le remplacement de portraits → "30% of customers buy stuff the art studio used to give away for free. Pure profit baby."

**Citations** : "Bottom Line: If a problem appears, and you can solve it immediately-in exchange for money-do it!" ; "Do you want fries with that? - McDonald's Famous Upsell"

**À retenir** : la math McDonald's - burger **$0.25** de profit (il faudrait "~10,000 burgers a day"), +frites **+$0.75**, +boisson en menu **+$1.75** (total $2.00, "an 8x increase"), +supersize **+$1** (total **$3.00**, "a 11.6x increase") [I - l'arithmétique ne se reconstitue pas : 0,25+0,75+1,75 = 2,75 ≠ 2,00, et 3,00/0,25 = 12x ≠ 11,6x ; chiffres du livre reproduits tels quels] - "the thing you sell the most isn't always the thing you make the most profit on. You make it on the second, third, and in the case of the burger business, fourth offers and beyond." ; "If McDonald's didn't upsell fries and soda, there wouldn't be a McDonald's."

### Menu Upsell (p.75)

**Concepts clés** : "you tell customers which options they don't need. Then, tell them what they do need, their preferences, and how to get their value from it." Combine jusqu'à 4 tactiques : **Unselling** ("you explain what they don't need as a way to get them excited about what they do") ; **Prescription Upselling** ("you explain how to use it as if they already have. Again, we remove the option of not buying") ; **A/B Upsell** ("Instead of asking if customers wanted to buy a product, yes or no, we ask which product they prefer: A or B. Either choice results in an upsell") ; **Card On File** ("you refer to ways they already have" - "it lowers the 'hidden costs' of buying").

**Frameworks & méthodes** : séquence en 4 étapes : unsell → prescribe → A/B → card on file. Dimensions A/B : "Quantity…, start dates…, payment preference…, flavors (chocolate or vanilla?), time slots…, media (read or listen?), delivery speeds…, sizes…, colors…, materials…, personnel…, communication (call or text?)." Nudge : "This is my favorite" / "X is usually a safe bet" ("If you want to move one particular product faster, nudge that one more."). Sold-out : "just collecting the cash and changing the delivery expectations." "Employees often like helping customers 'game the system.' Let them." "Unselling lower-margin stuff where appropriate incentivizes higher-margin upsells."

**Exemples** : Décembre 2013 - 19 consults sans vente, la 20e avec "a nice purse and a big diamond ring", **$5,000 d'inventaire** : "You've got a protein shake for breakfast, do you like chocolate or vanilla?" → "Which one's your favorite?" → "Chocolate." → "Great. I'll take one of those." → "After that conversation, I sold the next twenty customers in a row." ; Août 2014 - Prescription Upselling : instructions écrites sur le bon de commande ("Take one of these at night. Take two of these after lunch. Drink this after your workout.") → "my 30-day profits skyrocketed" (**$5,000-$10,000 de suppléments/mois**) ; Novembre 2016 - Unselling : "You're not trying to gain weight, right?"… "Later, I kept products just to cross them out!" ; scripts complets Massage Therapist, Dog Food, Digital Product.

**Citations** : "This took me ten years to learn. May you get the same value in ten minutes." ; "If you make it easy for people to buy, more people will."

**À retenir** : "when you give people the option to not buy, some don't buy. So, I give the option to pick between buying two similar things."

### Anchor Upsell (p.84)

**Concepts clés** : "With Anchor Upsells, you offer premium stuff first. If the customer gasps, you offer a cheaper but acceptable alternative." "The bigger the gasp, the more they bought." Primary vs secondary features : "Most people just want a suit. A few people want a fancy suit. A suit is the primary feature. The material, designer, etc. is secondary."

**Frameworks & méthodes** : 5 étapes : 1) "Present the Anchor-the really expensive thing" ; 2) "Get 'The Gasp'" ; 3) "Come to the rescue-ask if they care about what makes it premium" ; 4) "Present your main offer" ; 5) "Ask how they wanna pay-Which card do you prefer?" Règles : premium 5-10x plus cher ; mêmes primary features ; "If You Treat The Anchor Like A Fake, So Will The Customer" (le vendre réellement, passer à la suite "only after they pause, hesitate, or ask for something else") ; "Make A Premium Offer You Actually Want People To Buy" ; avoir l'alternative prête (le costumier "already had the 1/8th price suit pulled before my reaction") ; "if your customers don't gasp, then they probably find your premium offer reasonable…so just ask them if they wanna use the card on file."

**Exemples** : le costume **$16,000** → **$2,200** (budget $500, "I spent five times more than I had budgeted and felt OK about it" + $300 d'accessoires) ; lawn care **$1,000/semaine** → **$200/semaine** ; peinture **$1,000** → **$200** ; newsletter **$199/mois** → **$19/mois**. Ami de Gym Launch : "Tripling his profits" après avoir rendu l'ancre sincère.

**Citations** : "The only thing worse than making a $1,000 offer to a person with a $100 budget… is making a $100 offer to someone with a $1,000 budget." ; "After anchoring, offering the primary features for a fifth of the price makes the main offer a great deal."

**À retenir** : deux bonus cachés - "anchored customers spend a bit more than they plan to" et "some customers will buy the premium offer."

### Rollover Upsell (p.89)

**Concepts clés** : "Rollover Upsells credit some or all of a customer's previous purchases toward your next offer." Cadre Who/What/How : Who - (1) réengager les anciens clients (6+ mois), (2) sauver les clients mécontents avant le remboursement, (3) "rescue other people's upset customers", (4) upseller les clients réguliers ; What - "more of what they just got, something better, or something new and different" ; How - "apply all or part of the discount up front or spread it over time."

**Frameworks & méthodes** : prix : "make the upsell offer at least four times more than their rollover credit. So even if I apply the whole amount of the first purchase, it discounts 25% at most." Urgence : "A once-in-a-customer-lifetime offer. They don't get to sleep on it." Attirer de nouveaux clients : créditer ce qu'ils ont payé chez un concurrent - "scraping contact information from negative product reviews" et créer un forum de plaintes. "Do Rollover Upsells before refunding."

**Exemples** : Justin (juin 2014) : "We just give them fifty bucks off per month for a year" - "So even though they won their money back, they start paying immediately?" "Of course. I don't want people not paying." - "Boom. This was it. The missing link in my Money Model." ; winback : vidéos personnalisées pour **200 clients**, **$4,000 de crédit** chacun → ~**20%** de prise → ~**$1,900,000 de revenu annuel** en une journée d'enregistrement ; chiropracteur : "I want to give you $500 of your money back as credit" ; dentiste : crédit **$200** vers un package blanchiment ; software : "I'll credit whatever payments you have left with them to switch to ours."

**Citations** : "And although I learned the Rollover Upsell this way, you don't need a Win Your Money Back attraction offer to use it. You can Rollover Upsell anyone anything. (Even stuff people bought at other businesses…mwahaha)."

**À retenir** : "I don't want people not paying. ### Section IV - Downsell Offers (p.96) : règles du jeu

**Concepts clés** : "Downselling tweaks the original offer to find the highest value solution for the customer's budget. So any offer you make after someone says 'no' is a downsell." Deux dimensions : "I change how they pay or what they get." Les 5 règles : 1) "No means no for this thing, not no for everything." ; 2) "Downsells Are Trades… If you're gonna give something, get something." ; 3) "Personalize, Don't Pressure." ; 4) "Offer The Same Things In New Ways" ("a hundred different offers for the same product"); 5) "Don't Drop Your Price Just To Get Somebody To Buy… it's discounting. If someone wants what you have, and just doesn't want to pay the price-tough cookies." + "Customers Talk About Price" (tester les prix à l'avance ; "charging somebody less in the moment just because you felt scared of losing the sale" devient "an ethical problem").

**Exemples** : la boisson soda : "I could ask if they want a small, a juice, or a coffee. Am I being offensive by asking? Absolutely not."

**Citations** : "Everybody buys something." ; "Downsells give you another shot at getting a customer by turning nos into yeses."

### Payment Plan Downsells (p.100)

**Concepts clés** : "Instead of offering a different thing, I spread the cost by charging some of it up front and putting the rest into scheduled payments." Le vrai sens de "it costs too much" : "this costs too much up front." Pari à double tranchant : "They make you more money when you get more customers and those customers complete their payments. They make you less money when people cancel before you turn a profit. You lose the most when people who would have paid in full take a payment plan-and cancel early."

**Frameworks & méthodes** : 7 étapes ("I stop when they buy") : 1) récompenser le paiement intégral ("It's $15…but it's $10 if you prepay it. You save $5…that's what most people do" - "Same math, but feels better" + price anchor) ; 2) 3rd-party financing ("Car dealers do this all the time"), carte de crédit ("would you rather I decide your payment terms or you decide?"), layaway ("paying off the product before getting it" - "by far the most flexible for them and lowest risk for us") ; 3) Half Now Half Later : "When's the next time you get paid?" → "Wanna just put half down today and the rest down when you get paid?" ; 4) want-check 1-10 (8+ : "Awesome. Don't worry. We're gonna figure out a way to make this happen for you." / 7- : "Why not a 10?" puis Feature Downsell) ; 5) trois paiements ("⅓ now and ⅓ on the next two paychecks") ; 6) paiements étalés (Gym Launch : 16 semaines = 16 prélèvements hebdo) ; 7) Free Trial. Total : "makes up to nine offers." "Seesaw" Downselling : "Would you rather have giant monthly payments or tiny ones?" ; "pull your chair to their side of the table… The sale becomes a team effort." Upsell intégré : re-proposer le prepay discount pendant le plan ; "give your sales guys the same bonus to close the balance." Aligner les prélèvements sur les paydays (John) : "I often recoup a third of my declined payments." Règle de rentabilité : close rate en hausse MAIS "the same percentage of appointments paying in full" (ex. : 3 ventes → 6 ventes : cash up front des 3 premiers + paiements des 3 suivants). Données **Profitwell (14,000 entreprises)** : billing mensuel = **10.7%** de churn/mois, trimestriel = **5%**, annuel = **2%**. "I already present pricing in order of most cash up front to least… So start high (fewer bigger payments) and work your way down."

**Exemples** : août 2013 - premier payment plan : "OK, when do you get paid?" → "The first." → "Just give me your card and I'll charge it on the second. That work?" - "Yea-great!" "My first ever payment plan-a success. Hallelujah."

**Citations** : "Payment Plan Downsells work no matter how many zeroes the price tag has. I've made tens of millions of dollars with them and I still use them to this day." ; "Bottom Line: Changing how customers pay can have a massive difference in how long they stay."

**À retenir** : "if you give people the option to pay slower, they will pay slower. If you incentivize them to pay faster, they will pay faster."

### Trial With Penalty (p.107)

**Concepts clés** : "customers can try your product or service for free so long as they meet your terms" - "customers only pay if they don't meet the terms" (vs Win Your Money Back : "chance to get their money back if they meet the terms"). Positionnement : "you get it for free so long as you do this stuff…which makes you a perfect fit for my next offer." C'est un downsell : "I only downsell the trial if they say no to my first offer. And…it only changes what they pay today-not how much they pay in total." "I have only made this work in businesses where the customer has to do work to get results." Pairing : "Pay Less Now or Pay More Later as a downsell for physical products or one-time services… Trial With Penalty as a downsell for recurring products or services." "Just Call It A Trial" (jamais "penalty").

**Frameworks & méthodes** : 5 étapes : 1) offrir le trial en dernier ("Hmmm…that sure is a pickle…Let me get your ID and we can get the process started-fair enough?") ; 2) "Always Get A Credit Card" ("What card do you wanna use?" ; objection : "That's just how we've always done it.") ; 3) "Always Sell Staying And Paying" : "If this program got you the result, will you stay long-term?" Upsell selon l'issue : like → proposez plus ; hate → prenez le blâme ("Do not blame them… I can get about half of these people to buy") ; didn't use → "Reach out to people multiple times… Offer to waive the fee if they do… I don't like billing non-starters. A small fee isn't worth a 1-star review." Math : "close three of ten people on your Attraction Offer… downsell another four on a Trial With Penalty… upsell three of them. **Exemples** : Leila (printemps 2018) : onboarding HR gratuit si formation suivie, sinon payant - "It forced me to learn, and now I don't want to learn anyone else's complicated software…so we're sticking with them!" ; HR Software : **$500 d'onboarding** gratuit si "Attend Onboarding (3 Zooms de 60 min), Do the homework, Activate your employer profile, Get your employees set up by the end of the third call", puis **$99/mois** ; 28 Day Kick-That-Habit Blueprint (B2C) ; 5-Day Get Your First 5 Customers Challenge (B2B).

**À retenir** : diagnostics - personne ne prend le trial → baisser les exigences ; les gens ne suivent pas → mieux expliquer les fees ; personne ne reste → améliorer le back-end.

### Feature Downsells (p.115)

**Concepts clés** : "Feature Downsells lower prices by changing what customers get. I do them by offering lesser quantity, lower quality, lower price alternatives, or cutting optional components." Formule : "take something away, lower the price, and in so many words ask 'how about now?'" "All features have a price and a value." "If you remove stuff they hate, and lower the price a lot, they get a better deal. If you remove stuff they love, and lower the price a little, they get a worse deal. Both get people to buy." Ordre de retrait : "remove features from highest to lowest value" pour que le client se "re-upsell" lui-même. "Business terrorists" : "People who demand to pay less for the same thing… I don't negotiate with terrorists." Position Helpful Guide : "If you stay a helpful guide, you can downsell as many offers as necessary without exhausting the customer." The Minimum : "so nothing more than the minimum package then?" (say no to say yes).

**Frameworks & méthodes** : levier de qualité de service complet : Time Availability, Location Availability, Cancellations, Speed Of Response, Speed Of Delivery, Service Ratio (one-on-one vs one-to-many), Communication Method, Provider Qualifications, Live vs Recorded, In-Person vs Remote, **DIY/DWY/DFY**, Expirations, Personalization, Insurance/Guarantee. DFY → DIY : chiropracteur ("let's start you with some tools you can use to do it yourself at home?"), peintre (peinture + location de machine à spray "for a daily rate"), Hormozi : "Instead of me and my team buying your company and actively growing your business, why don't you just attend a workshop?" Process standardisé : "First, I cut something valuable and lower the price a little… If that fails, I continue removing features and lowering prices until they buy. I'd rather people get something rather than nothing." Nommer les combos : "The Whale Package," "The Total Transformation," "High Roller" - "Look at airlines. Make your version of First Class→Business Class→Economy." Température check après 2 refus (1-10 ; 8+ → Payment Plan Downsells ; 7- → "What would a 10 look like?" puis recomposer) - "you can alternate between payment plan and feature downsells. When you use both, you become very difficult to refuse." Après chaque downsell : "Deal?" ou "Fair Enough?" Free orientations : "Of the people who showed up to the orientation (about half), almost all of them bought supplements." Downseller la garantie elle-même. Downseller les clients actuels avant qu'ils n'annulent (2e segment le plus précieux). Barter : "I'll knock $100 off if you: 1) Leave me a review on all review sites 2) Leave me a video testimonial 3) Make a public social post…showing your progress 4) Introduce me to two friends… Deal?" - "To me, the advertising is worth more than the $100 discount. **Exemples** : l'ami (2019) : suppression de la garantie "tripled my close rate from 25% to 75%… more people bought the main thing" - "Before, I only had one full-price option. So if 100 people got on a call, 25 bought. Now, 35 people buy the main thing and 40 take the downsell." Script : "If you don't want the option to get your money back, you can pay less. **Citations** : "I lower the price, but I justify it by cutting a feature. That way, I'm not discounting." ; "they only see the value of the guarantee after you remove it" ; "When people have a product they like at a price they find fair, they keep paying."

**À retenir** : conclusion Section IV - "it's less about having a hundred different products with the same offer, and more about having a hundred different offers for the same product. But, no matter what, the offer is never the same stuff for cheaper."

### Section V - Continuity Offers (p.123)

**Concepts clés** : "Continuity Offers provide ongoing value that customers make ongoing payments for-until they cancel." "you sell once, but get paid again and again." Mathématique exacte : "you offer a $1,000 thing to 100 people and 10 buy-you make $10,000 (10 x $1,000)" vs "$50 per month instead. At fifty bucks, we can get 40 out of 100 to buy… kept for twenty months… You go from making $10,000 now and $0 over time to $2,000 now and $40,000 over time" + "four times the customers to upsell later." Contrepartie : "you make way less money now. That makes it tough to use as an Attraction Offer on its own." La solution Hormozi : "I make them last" - Attraction profitable → Upsell/Downsell → Continuity → "upsell a bulk amount of time or product at a discount" → "they automatically enter continuity after they've used up their bulk purchase." John : "You can shear a sheep for a lifetime, but you can only skin it once."

**Citations** : "The only thing better than getting someone to buy once, is getting them to buy again." ; "Continuity Offers work with rewards or punishment. I prefer rewards." ; "All offers depend on getting customers to buy. But, Continuity Offers depend on getting customers to keep buying."

### Continuity Bonus Offers (p.126)

**Concepts clés** : "you give the customer an awesome thing if they sign up today. Typically, the bonus itself has more value than the first continuity payment." "anything you offer for free you can also offer as a discount… we want to do both." Publicité : "'Join my membership program' isn't nearly as compelling as 'get this free valuable thing.'" Bonus = types d'upsell : more of the same / complementary / upgrade. "If the bonus is too different you will attract the wrong customers." Bonus réalistes : "Some business owners make up ridiculous values. Don't do this." Titres comme bonus : "silver, gold, diamond, double diamond" après 3/6/12 mois - "she found her customers cared more about the title than any other bonus."

**Frameworks & méthodes** : séquence d'ancrage : vendre les bénéfices du bonus d'abord → ancrer ("It may shock them-and that's okay") → "Do you want to know how you can get this for free?" → "Become a VIP member today and you'll get it all as a free gift for joining. Or, you can just buy it for $XXX-which would you prefer?" → empiler "amazing thing 1, amazing thing 2, amazing thing 3" avec les valeurs en dollars. Tableau standalone vs continuity (sur l'exemple $199/mois) : 50% de choix continuity → standalone **1.33x** ($399 = $266/mois) ; 60% → **1.66x** ($499 = $333/mois) ; 70% → **2x** ($599 = $399/mois) ; 80% → **2.33x** ($699 = $466/mois) ; 90% → **2.66x** ($799 = $532/mois). "people pay 33% more to avoid continuity." Bulk prepaid : "'buy five months get one free.' Only one out of every eight people has to take the upsell to raise 30-day profits by 50%!" Engagements : "only allow customers to get the bonus if they commit to 3-6-12+ months" - "This nets fewer sales, but more committed customers."

**Exemples** : le gym owner (automne 2019) : avant - "thirty-four out of a hundred… convert half (seventeen) to stay" ; après - "fifteen to sign up for the challenge but we get forty to go straight into continuity. And of those forty, about eight… take the six month prepayment upsell" → "you tripled membership sales" ; pet food : "every dog toy we've ever made for free, an $800 value, when you sign up for monthly dog food shipments for $59 per month" ; accelerator : "$1,000 on its own. Get it free when you become a member for $100 per month" ; newsletter : "40 newsletters valued at $15,880… $399/mo after a 30-day free trial" + "lock in a lifetime discount to $299 per month."

**Citations** : "Stacking bonuses this way gets even more people to join your continuity." ; "This strategy often lowers the cost of getting a customer more than the cost of the bonus. And that's the point."

### Continuity Discount Offers (p.134)

**Concepts clés** : "you give products or services away for free if the customer commits to buying more products and services over time." Quatre modes d'application : "Up front, at the end, an even spread, or after the first month or two."

**Frameworks & méthodes** : **Up Front** : réduction appliquée d'emblée, terme repoussé - "best in industries that have a successful history of enforcing contracts (cell phones, storage, real estate, equipment, or anything with collateral)" ; "if you have historically high churn, then skip this one" ; "this does not get customers profitably. It gets customers, but delays cash." **At The End** : "So long as they make every payment on time…they get a bonus time equal to the value of the discount. They earn their free time." **Spread Over Time** : "three months free for a one-year commitment. At $200 per month you've discounted $600… a $600/12 months = $50 discount each month" + option "keep the discount for life after the term is over" si tous les paiements à l'heure. **After the first 1-2 payments** : "I prefer to do it by presenting the offer as 'first and last month,' 'last month up front,' or adding some sort of activation fee" (valide la carte bancaire). **★ "Highest Value Per Word Note In This Book" ★ - Bill weekly** : "There are 12 months in a year, but the year has 13 four-week cycles. That's an 8.3% difference." Offrir "$100 every four weeks" (vs "$100 a month") : même nombre de clients, **+8.3% de revenu annuel** ; "if your business has 20% margins, this skyrockets annual profit by 41%" ; "This has literally made me millions in pure profit. So yeah, do it." **Extend, don't cut** : "pay 12 months and get three free (15 total months)" plutôt que "pay for nine months then get three free (12 total months)" - puis Feature Downsell un terme plus court. **3% processing fee** : "Yea, it's $X plus a 3% processing fee" - "I've never had anyone not buy because of a processing fee… If you run a 10% profit business, and add 3%, you just added 30% to your profit." **Deux formes de paiement** : "Do you want to save the processing fee?…Give us a second form of payment" + "Get ACH If You Can." **Gift cards** : appliquable "after the first three payments or so", offertable à un ami ("now you've got a lead magnet!") - "many people simply forget to use it. In that instance, you just got a full-priced sign-up." **Lifetime discount au point de churn** : "Make X the month your average customer drops off" (ex. : mois 4). **Cancellation policy** : décidée à l'avance (30/60 jours, frais, cancel anytime) ; préférée : "make the cancellation fee equal to the discount they agreed to get. So if they got $600 in discounts… they can pay $600… This puts them back at the month-to-month rate." "Make Sure Customers Know How To Cancel… Small businesses don't get rich by making stuff hard for their customers." **Exit interview** : "Some people like to vent. Let them. Get more angry about the problem than them… Sometimes, they will save themselves!" ; "I routinely save a third of customers that agree to exit interviews" ; "I'll waive your cancellation fee if you come in and tell me what I could do better."

**Exemples** : l'éboueur (printemps 2018, Austin) : "I went to all the big apartments and said I'd do their trash for a whole year free if they contracted me to do the next five years paid" - "after that one year mark passed, the cash came floodin'… I sold the whole shebang for a pretty penny." "There's cash in trash baby, what can I say." ; la compagnie de riz : 3 options - one-time price / 5% off subscription / **15% off si 5 mois consécutifs** ("You earned the lifetime lower rate. I'm sure they figured out that it was just beyond where most people canceled").

**Citations** : "Frontloaded discounts convert more customers, but may have higher churn. Backloading discounts converts fewer customers, but they lower churn." ; "What else can you do legally that makes so much money for so little work?"

### Waived Fee Offer (p.142)

**Concepts clés** : "you ask the customer to pay a startup fee as part of joining a month-to-month program. Typically, I do 3-5x my monthly rate. Then, you offer to discount the entire fee if they commit longer term. But, if they cancel inside the term, they pay the fee." "We take a greater risk if they pay month-to-month. But they take a greater risk if they commit." "Bottom Line: Customers will stay longer if leaving costs more than staying." "Fees Get Them To Start… People want to avoid fees." "Fees Get Them To Stick… if the cost to quit exceeds the cost to stay, they will probably stay."

**Frameworks & méthodes** : exemple complet : commitment **12 mois**, taux **$1,000/mois**, fee **$5,000** - Option A : $5,000 + $1,000 pour le premier mois, cancel anytime ; Option B : fee annulée si commitment de 12 mois, le fee n'est payé qu'en cas de rupture anticipée. Justification : "It costs us money to get you started. If you only wanna test us out, you cover those costs. If you commit longer, I'll cover them." Règles : commitment minimum **1 an** ("The longer the commitment, the better this works" - "especially well with services that take a long time to work (SEO, Investing, Weight Loss, etc.)") ; "If More Than 5% Of People Want To Cancel Early, Look Into It" ("You want to nudge them, not handcuff people into paying for something they hate") ; plus de cash up front → fee plus petit **1.5-3x** le taux mensuel ; "Drop The Fee After The Customer Fulfills The Commitment… they have earned their free cancellation. This makes it equitable." ; variante "cause" : "What cause do you absolutely hate?...Great. If you cancel early, I will be donating your setup fee to them."

**Exemples** : le vendeur high-ticket légendaire (janvier 2021) : "I tell customers they have two options: 'You can go month-to-month with a big setup fee… Or, if you commit to a year, I'll waive the fee.' And I make the fee huge, so buyers commit to avoid it. I also have them initial that they understand they can quit early if they pay the fee I waived." - "It costs a lot to quit in the beginning, so that keeps them engaged" ; "once they pass that point, it costs about the same to cancel as it does to stick it out. So, they just stick it out." "Bingo." - "I'd rather make a few million bucks a year with zero employees and cool customers than build some gigantic business… I just collect monthly payments and chill."

### Section VI - Make Your Money Model (p.147)

**Concepts clés** : définition finale : "A Money Model is a deliberate sequence of offers. It's what you offer, when you offer, and how you offer it to make as much money as you can as fast as you can. Ideally, to make enough money from one customer to get and service at least two more customers in less than 30 days." Good Money Model : "makes more profit from a customer than it costs to get and service them in the first 30 days. That's the bare minimum." $100M Money Model : "makes more profit from one customer than it costs to get and service many customers in the first 30 days, which removes cash as a limiter to scaling your business." 3 étapes : "Stage I: Get Cash-Attraction Offers get more customers for less ; Stage II: Get More Cash-Upsell & Downsell Offers make more money from them faster ; Stage III: Get The Most Cash-Continuity Offers maximize their total money spent." "Money Model growth happens alongside the growth of the business" - un modèle "fini" posé d'un coup sur une entreprise bootstrap "will collapse on top of you." ## Frameworks & Modèles

### Money Model (3 stages / 5 steps)
- **Formule** : Stage I Get Cash (Attraction) → Stage II Get More Cash (Upsell + Downsell) → Stage III Get The Most Cash (Continuity). Évolution : customers reliably → pay for themselves → pay for other customers → max LTV → "spend as many advertising dollars as I can."
- **Quand** : toute entreprise, dès qu'une offre d'acquisition fonctionne de façon fiable.
- **Exemple** : Gym Launch : $476K → $1.5M → $2.3M → $4.4M/mois.
- **Pièges** : implémenter tout d'un coup ("it will collapse on top of you") ; mesurer en semaines au lieu de quarters.

### Win Your Money Back
- **Formule** : "If you do x within y time within z rules, you can get it free." Critères : Results / Actions / Actions+Results. Taux de remboursement < 5% requis. Store credit étalé ($600/12 mois = $50/mois).
- **Quand** : produits de transformation (départs/abandons fréquents : fitness, skills, business, santé).
- **Exemple** : Danny, $500/8 semaines, 13 références.
- **Pièges** : pas d'upsell prêt pour appliquer les gains ; ~10% des clients demandent le remboursement.

### Giveaway Offer
- **Formule** : 6 étapes (Grand Prize → promotional offer → contacts/éligibilité → qualifying actions → deadline 3-7 jours → annonce gagnant + contact privé des autres, claim sous 7 jours). Discount 10-30% des gross margins ; annoncer la pleine valeur (cost-to-value : 10% → 64%).
- **Quand** : pour générer des leads qualifiés sur votre offre la plus chère.
- **Exemple** : certification fitness "full-ride scholarship" ; consulting $12,000/$6,000.
- **Pièges** : Grand Prize pas assez "grand" (billets d'événement) ; pas de downsell ; urgence/claim absents.

### Decoy Offer
- **Formule** : annoncer la version inférieure (sans garantie) → présenter les deux, mettre l'accent sur le premium → "Are you here for free stuff or lasting results?" → contrast huge.
- **Quand** : coûts d'acquisition en hausse, show-up faible (passer à un Discount Offer).
- **Exemple** : $399 Ultimate, 8/10 de prise ; tanning $19.99 vs $25 day pass.
- **Pièges** : decoy trop proche du premium ; vendre des features au lieu des bénéfices.

### Buy X Get Y Free
- **Formule** : "when customers buy something, they get other stuff free" - toujours plus de gratuit que de payant ; monter les prix durablement avant.
- **Quand** : acheteurs uniques (touristes) ; cash rapide (10% des clients existants).
- **Exemple** : Boot Factory $600 = 3 paires de $200 ; "Buy 6 Months Get 12 Months Free."
- **Pièges** : vendre ce qu'on ne peut pas livrer ; ne pas budgéter le service prépayé.

### Pay Less Now or Pay More Later
- **Formule** : Pay Later (carte enregistrée, $0, garantie conditionnelle, cancel possible) → Pay Now (20-50% de réduction + bonus) ; si >10% d'annulations, promesse trop forte/critères trop bas/prix trop haut.
- **Quand** : produits physiques et services one-time (comme downsell).
- **Exemple** : $297 débité demain ou $97 maintenant ; hedges $599/$369.
- **Pièges** : promesse vague (pas un "yes/no" clair) ; garantie inconditionnelle.

### Classic Upsell
- **Formule** : "You can't have X without Y!" - résoudre le problème suivant au moment où il apparaît ; offrir le plus rentable d'abord ; 4 bonus de réserve ; BAMFAM ; monétiser garanties (5-50%).
- **Quand** : chaque business avec une seule offre ("You barely have a business-you have a front end").
- **Exemple** : McDonald's $0.25 → $3.00 (11.6x) ; art studio +10% → 30% de prise.
- **Pièges** : ne jamais demander ("The worst thing is if they would've said yes but you never asked").

### Menu Upsell
- **Formule** : Unsell → Prescribe → A/B → Card On File. Nudge une ligne ; unseller les bas margins.
- **Quand** : multiples offres disponibles.
- **Exemple** : chocolat ou vanille → 20 clients d'affilée ; instructions écrites = prescription.
- **Pièges** : demander "oui/non" au lieu de A/B ; instructions vagues.

### Anchor Upsell
- **Formule** : premium 5-10x d'abord → The Gasp → "Come to the rescue" → main offer (mêmes primary features) → "Which card do you prefer?"
- **Quand** : toute vente en personne ou à fort ticket.
- **Exemple** : costume $16,000 → $2,200 (5x le budget) ; newsletter $199/$19.
- **Pièges** : traiter l'ancre comme un faux ("If You Treat The Anchor Like A Fake, So Will The Customer") ; changer les primary features.

### Rollover Upsell
- **Formule** : créditer les achats passés (les siens ou ceux d'un concurrent) ; offre ≥ 4x le crédit (≤ 25% de discount) ; one-time only ; winback 6+ mois.
- **Quand** : réengagement, sauvetage pré-remboursement, vol de clients mécontents des concurrents.
- **Exemple** : Justin $50/mois × 12 ; 200 vidéos → 20% → $1.9M/an.
- **Pièges** : créditer trop (discount > 25%) ; pas d'urgence.

### Payment Plan Downsell (7 steps / 9 offers)
- **Formule** : prepay discount → 3rd-party financing/carte/layaway → half now half later (paydays) → want-check 1-10 → trois paiements → étalement → Free Trial. "I stop when they buy."
- **Quand** : "I can't afford it" = "too much up front".
- **Exemple** : "When do you get paid?" → "The first." → charge le 2 (loyer le 5) ; Gym Launch 16 prélèvements hebdo.
- **Pièges** : paiement intégral en baisse (on met des full-payers sur plans) ; churn par cadence (10.7%/5%/2% - Profitwell).

### Trial With Penalty
- **Formule** : downsell en dernier → carte D'ABORD → "If this program got you the result, will you stay long-term?" → fees expliquées APRÈS → check-ins obligatoires ; fees par manquement ($50 × 10) plutôt que lump ; recharger les échecs : 1→2→4.
- **Quand** : récurrent, où le client doit travailler pour obtenir des résultats.
- **Exemple** : Leila/HR software $500 onboarding ; 3 ventes → 6 ventes (doublé).
- **Pièges** : appeler ça "penalty" ; facturer les non-starters ("A small fee isn't worth a 1-star review").

### Feature Downsell
- **Formule** : "take something away, lower the price, and ask 'how about now?'" - retirer du plus au moins valorisé ; nommer les packages (Whale/Total Transformation/Minimum) ; "Deal?"/"Fair Enough?" ; température check ; alterner avec Payment Plans ; barter (4 actions = $100 off).
- **Quand** : quand le prix doit baisser au total (jamais "just because").
- **Exemple** : garantie retirée → close rate 25% → 75% ; "35 buy the main thing and 40 take the downsell."
- **Pièges** : "business terrorists" ("I don't negotiate with terrorists") ; pousser au lieu de guider.

### Continuity Bonus Offer
- **Formule** : bonus > premier paiement ; ancrage bonus d'abord → "Do you want to know how you can get this for free?" → stack avec valeurs en dollars ; tableau 1.33x-2.66x (50→90%) ; "buy five months get one free" (1/8 des clients = +50% de profit 30 jours).
- **Quand** : toute offre récurrente.
- **Exemple** : gym owner 34/17 → 15/40/8 = triplé ; dog toys $800/$59.
- **Pièges** : bonus non liés au cœur d'offre ; valeurs d'ancrage ridicules.

### Continuity Discount Offer
- **Formule** : 4 modes (up front / at the end / spread / après 1-2 paiements) ; ★ bill weekly (+8.3% annuel, +41% de profit à 20% de marges) ★ ; extend the term ; +3% processing fee ; 2e forme de paiement/ACH ; gift cards ; lifetime discount au point de churn ; cancellation fee = discount reçu ; exit interview (1/3 sauvés).
- **Quand** : récurrent avec terme long.
- **Exemple** : l'éboueur (1 an gratuit / 5 ans payés) ; riz 5%/15%.
- **Pièges** : Up Front discount dans un secteur à fort churn ; rendre l'annulation difficile.

### Waived Fee Offer
- **Formule** : fee startup 3-5x le taux mensuel, annulé si commitment ≥ 1 an, payé seulement en cas de rupture ; fee 1.5-3x pour plus de cash up front ; >5% d'annulations précoces → investigate ; fee disparaît après le terme.
- **Quand** : services long-cycle (SEO, investing, weight loss) ; "Customers will stay longer if leaving costs more than staying."
- **Exemple** : $5,000 fee / $1,000/mois / 12 mois ; le "legendary high-ticket seller."
- **Pièges** : "handcuff people into paying for something they hate" ("You want to nudge them, not handcuff").

## Leçons actionnables (checklist)

**Attraction**
- [ ] Choisir UNE Attraction Offer et la rendre fiable avant toute autre étape ("measure in quarters").

- [ ] Giveaway : Grand Prize = l'offre que tout le monde doit acheter, valeur annoncée ; discount 10-30% des margins ; 3-7 jours ; claim 7 jours ; "someone actually has to win."
- [ ] Decoy : version inférieure sans garantie, contraste maximum, vendre les bénéfices ; si show-up faible, passer de Free à Discount Offer.

- [ ] Pay Less Now or Pay More Later : promesse "yes/no" mesurable ; carte enregistrée ; Pay Now à 20-50% + bonus ; si >10% d'annulations, corriger.

**Upsell**
- [ ] Ajouter le "fries with that" de votre business : plus / mieux / complémentaire.
- [ ] Menu Upsell dans l'ordre : unsell → prescribe → A/B → card on file ; nudger les produits à pousser.

## Limites & critiques

- **Résultats non typiques** : le disclaimer légal du livre lui-même avertit que les performances sont hypothétiques, préparées avec le recul, sans risque financier réel ; la trajectoire $43M/66%/$100M de Hormozi n'est pas reproductible telle quelle.
- **Données auto-rapportées et anecdotes** : les ratios (1.33x-2.66x, 8/10 de prise, 1/3 de remboursements récupérés, 1/3 d'exit interviews sauvés, 20% de winback) reposent sur des tests personnels non audités ; le chiffre "$1,000 into $10,000,000 in ten months" semble être une coquille ou une simplification.
- **Mathématiques approximatives** : le "10% discount → 64% cost-to-value" (calcul : $5,000/$1,800 ≈ 64% mais 10% de $2,000 = 11.1% réel), le "doubler les clients" du Trial With Penalty et le "1 sur 8 = +50% de profit 30 jours" supposent des marges et volumes implicites non précisés.
- **Manipulation assumée** : "This section is brutally effective and must be used ethically." Les techniques (fees expliquées APRÈS la carte, "assumed close", "you don't want anything else do you?", donation à une cause détestée) flirtent avec des zones grises éthiques et légales ; la légalité (notamment "free" et les conditions d'annulation) varie par juridiction - le livre renvoie lui-même aux avocats.
- **Applicabilité limitée** : Trial With Penalty "only work[s] in businesses where the customer has to do work to get results" ; les Giveaways/Anchor/face-to-face conviennent mal aux modèles purement en ligne et automatisés ; le Card On File et les payday plans supposent des prélèvements sur carte et des cultures de paiement par chèque de paie bihebdomadaire.
- **Contradictions assumées** : "Any Offer Can Be Used On Its Own, At Any Time, in Any Order" vs la structure stricte en 3 étapes ; "don't discount" vs "prepay discounts", "price-test ahead of time" vs "raise until you make less money" - le livre privilégie la souplesse au cadre formel.
